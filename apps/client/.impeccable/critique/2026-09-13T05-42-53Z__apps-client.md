## Overall Impression

You have a genuinely strong CV and five genuinely good essays sitting inside someone else's demo. The single biggest opportunity is not visual: **the site never tells a visitor who you are.** A recruiter opening this on a phone sees a 48px logo (the name is `hidden ... sm:block` in `Header.tsx:18`), the word "Latest," and the sentence "a web developer who loves to write about lifestyles" — while `timelineData.ts` holds Agoda, JAIST AI-safety research, SCBX, and a 3.98 GPA at Chula, three screens down behind an unexplained `<h2>Now</h2>`.

"Modernize and simplify" here is mostly **subtraction and identification**, not restyling. Delete the template residue, delete the second blog index, delete the tag system, fix what's broken — then spend the reclaimed budget on one real idea: a homepage that says your name.

## What's Working

1. **The date/reading-time rail in `BlogCard.tsx:16-27`.** `xl:grid xl:grid-cols-4 xl:items-baseline` puts a formatted date and "2 min read" in a left rail, baseline-aligned to the title. It sets time-expectation before commitment and gives the list an editorial spine instead of a stack of cards. This is the one piece of layout doing real work — and `ListLayout.tsx:142-177` re-implements it by hand instead of importing it, dropping the "Read more →" affordance on the way.

2. **The `####` closing-takeaway convention.** Every essay resolves into one earned sentence — `disappointed-moment.mdx:19`, `teaching-failure.mdx:21`. This is a writing pattern you invented, it lands, and it is the only place the site has a voice. It currently renders as an `<h4>`: semantically a lie, visually indistinguishable from a section header.

3. **Dark mode is actually complete.** `Header`, `Footer`, `MobileNav`, `Timeline`, `ProjectCard`, `prism.css`, and `typography.invert` all have dark variants, and `theme-provider.tsx:10` correctly defaults to `system` with `suppressHydrationWarning`. Most forks ship a half-broken dark mode; this one doesn't.

4. **`timelineData.ts` as raw material.** Specific, quantified, and honest ("GPA 3.56. Played Football all day. Started coding for the first time"). The content for a genuinely persuasive About page already exists in the repo.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Broken links and `undefined` ship on your public share surfaces
**What.** Four verified defects, all live:
- `BlogWithDetail.tsx:114` — `href="twitter.com/TODO"`. `Link.tsx:10-21` branches on `startsWith("/")` and `startsWith("#")`; this matches neither, so it becomes `<a target="_blank" href="twitter.com/TODO">`, a scheme-less relative URL resolving to `nacnano.dev/blogs/<slug>/twitter.com/TODO`. A guaranteed 404, in a new tab, on **every post**.
- `seo.tsx:21` — `description: \`${description} | ${siteMetadata.description}\``, but `description` is optional and `/blogs`, `/projects`, `/about` all call `genPageMetaData` without it. The OG description for those pages literally begins `undefined | `.
- `sitemap.ts:15` lists `"blog"` (route is `/blogs`) and omits `/about`; `rss.mjs:12,14` emits `/blog/${slug}` so every RSS item 404s.
- `dating-app.mdx:11,39` render `TODO: add Tinder ads` and `TODO: Add a photo of me` as visible published paragraphs.

**Why it matters.** Paste this URL into LinkedIn or a recruiter DM and the preview card reads "undefined | These are blogs written by Nacnano…". The rest tells every technical visitor you ship unfinished work — and your audience reads repos, because `BlogWithDetail.tsx:118` links them to one on every post.

**Fix.** Delete the "Discuss on Twitter" branch (`:113-121`) or build a real intent URL from `siteMetadata.twitter`. Guard `seo.tsx:21` with `description ?? siteMetadata.description`. Fix `"blog"` → `"blogs"` in `sitemap.ts:15`, add `about`; fix `/blog/` → `/blogs/` in `rss.mjs`. Strip the two TODO lines from `dating-app.mdx`.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P0] Every page force-downloads a 3.5 MB PNG to render a 48px logo
**What.** `Logo.tsx:14-19` stacks two independent oversizing bugs on a component that lives in the root layout:
- `blurDataURL={imgSrc}` points at `/static/images/logo.png`, which is **3,514,990 bytes at 1928×1928**. `blurDataURL` must be a data URI; passing a real path makes Next inject `background-image: url(<original>)`, so the browser fetches the raw unoptimized original *in addition to* the optimized source.
- `width="0" height="0" sizes="100vw"` tells Next to generate and serve a full-viewport-width srcset (up to 3840w on retina) for a 48px slot.

Same `blurDataURL` bug at `AuthorLayout.tsx:32` (733 KB avatar), `BlogWithDetail.tsx:80`, and `ProjectCard.tsx:24,34`. Separately, `siteMetadata.socialBanner` is `oong-oong.jpg` at **2,360,653 bytes** — the OG card for every page on the site.

**Why it matters.** This is the entire mobile experience on a Thai 4G connection: ~6 MB of images before a word of text on the homepage. It is also the cheapest fix on this list.
**Fix.** Resize `logo.png` to ~96px and `oong-oong.jpg` to 1200×630. Give `Logo` explicit `width`/`height` and drop `sizes="100vw"`. Remove every `placeholder="blur"` + path-valued `blurDataURL` pair, or generate real base64 data URIs. Delete the 5 orphaned images (~666 KB): `time-machine.jpg`, `cugetreg-full.png`, `google.png`, `sparrowhawk-avatar.jpg`, `cugetreg-logo.svg`.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable optimize`

### [P1] The About timeline is least legible on the device recruiters use
**What.** `Timeline.tsx:19-26` renders every row as `flex items-center` with a fixed `w-1/2 pr-8 text-right` date column and `w-1/2 pl-8` content column — **at every breakpoint, with no `md:` variant**. On 375px that is ~139px of usable text width per side. "Japan Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (JAIST), Nomi, Japan" wraps into a ~7-line ribbon beside a 4-line date. `items-center` also floats the dot (`:26`, `top-1/2`) to the vertical middle of a 7-line entry, decoupled from the date it anchors.

**Why it matters.** This is the CV. It is the reason a recruiter is on the site, it is the only fully authored layout in the repo, and it degrades worse on mobile than anything the template shipped.

**Fix.** Below `md`, collapse to a single left rail (line at `left-4`, date stacked above title, no right-align). Above `md`, keep the split but switch `items-center` → `items-start` and pin the dot to the first line. Then restructure the content: group the 12 entries into **Work** / **Education**, collapse pre-2022 behind "Show earlier," and lead with JAIST / Agoda / SCBX instead of a chronological stream that opens on a 2016 secondary-school GPA.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P1] There is no type system, and the accent color fails AA
**What.** Verified WCAG math on the shipped hex values:
- `primary-500` `#a855f7` on white = **3.96:1** — below the 4.5:1 AA floor. That is every prose link (`tailwind.config.js:39`), every tag (`Tag.tsx:12`), "Read more →", "All Blogs →", "Learn more →". The hover state `primary-600` passes at 5.38:1 — so links only become readable once you point at them.
- `gray-400` on white for reading time (`BlogCard.tsx:24`, `ListLayout.tsx:150`) = **~2.5:1**. Fails in both themes.
- `Space_Grotesk` is instantiated at `layout.tsx:18` but `space_grotesk.variable` is **never applied to any element** (`:73` passes only `inter.variable`), and `tailwind.config.js:26` has it commented out. Both halves are dead — yet `next/font/google` still emits the `@font-face` block and downloads the files.
- `BlogWithDetail.tsx:110` uses `prose max-w-none`, yielding ~90 characters per line at 16px — well past comfortable measure for reflective essays.

Net: the entire "design system" is Inter at two sizes with one weight jump. Every page `h1` is the identical string `text-3xl ... md:text-6xl font-extrabold` (`Main.tsx:12`, `ListLayout.tsx:61`, `AuthorLayout.tsx:21`, `projects/page.tsx:12`, `tags/page.tsx:21`).

**Why it matters.** On a site whose purpose is reading, links failing AA and metadata at 2.5:1 are a functional defect. And the absence of a second face, a defined measure, a radius token, or a shadow language is precisely *why* it reads as a template — there is nothing to read it as instead.

**Fix.** Move the resting link color to `purple-600` (keeps the hue, clears AA), reserve `purple-500` for dark mode. Raise metadata to `gray-600` / `dark:gray-400`. Then commit: either attach `space_grotesk.variable` and uncomment `tailwind.config.js:26` for a display face, or delete the dead import and pick a serif for essay body. Set `prose-lg max-w-[68ch]` on the post body.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable typeset`

### [P2] The tag system is noise, and the committed tag artifact is template-only
**What.** Two layered problems.
- The **checked-in** `src/app/tag-data.json` contains only demo-post tags — `next-js: 5`, `guide: 5`, `tailwind: 3`, `canada`, `hello`, `markdown` — and `reflection: 1` when five published posts carry it. None of the real tags (`romance`, `food`, `lifestyle`, `teaching`) appear. `contentlayer.config.ts:55` only excludes drafts when `NODE_ENV === "production"`, so any dev or non-prod build overwrites it with the template's tags. `rss.mjs` imports this file to generate per-tag feeds.
- Even regenerated correctly, it is **10 tags for 5 posts, 9 of them singletons**, and six of those come from `towrite-list.mdx` — a numbered list of topics you *haven't written yet*. `/tags/music` returns one post containing nothing about music.

**Why it matters.** `Tags` occupies 1 of 4 desktop nav slots — 25% of your navigation — pointing at a page that is either template residue or a list of dead ends. Highest-cost, lowest-value element in the IA.
**Fix.** Remove `{ href: "/tags" }` from `headerNavLinks.ts:10` and delete the tag block at `ListLayout.tsx:91-129`. Collapse to 2–3 real categories. If tags stay: move the draft filter out of the `isProduction` conditional and gitignore `tag-data.json` so a stale artifact can never ship.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

### [P2] Keyboard and screen-reader users hit dead ends
**What.** `focus-visible` appears **0 times** across `src/`; `tailwind.css` defines no global focus style.
- `ListLayout.tsx:120-126` — `<div onClick={...}>` for "Show all tags". No `role`, no `tabIndex`, no key handler. Unreachable by keyboard, invisible to AT.
- `not-found.tsx:20` — `focus:outline-none` removes the outline, and its replacement `focus:shadow-outline-blue` is a **Tailwind v1 class that does not exist in v3** and is not defined in the config. The indicator is deleted with nothing restoring it.
- `MobileNav.tsx:41-79` — the panel is always in the DOM, hidden only by `translate-x-full`, with no `hidden` / `inert` / `aria-hidden`. **Off-screen nav links stay in the tab order while the menu is closed.**
- `social-icons/index.tsx:44` — `<span className="sr-only">{href}</span>`. Screen readers announce "https://www.facebook.com/chotpisit.adunsehawat/" instead of "Facebook," twelve times per session (footer + about).
- No skip link; `layout.tsx:105`'s `<main>` has no `id`.
- Heading order: `ListLayout.tsx` goes h1 → h3 → h3 (h2 skipped, and the h3s are *filter controls* styled as headings); `AuthorLayout.tsx` goes h1 → h3 → h2 (inverted).

**Why it matters.** Zero focus styling plus a non-button control means the tag toggle is simply unusable without a mouse, and the mobile nav traps tab focus in invisible links on every page.
**Fix.** Global `:focus-visible` ring in `tailwind.css`. `<div onClick>` → `<button>`. Add `inert`/`hidden` to the closed `MobileNav` panel. Replace `sr-only={href}` with the icon `kind`. Add a skip link and `id="main"`. Fix heading order; demote the filter "headings" to spans.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

## Questions to Consider

1. **Why does `/blogs` exist?** Five posts, and `/` already lists all five. You spend a nav slot, a route, a `ListLayout`, a search field, a tag cloud, and a `Pagination` component to render the homepage twice. Delete it, make `/` the archive, reclaim the slot for Résumé.
2. **The `####` closing line is the best idea in this repo — why is it a heading tag?** That deserves a designed pull-quote component, and it is the one place the accent color would genuinely earn its keep.
3. **Who is this site for, and are you willing to say so?** Recruiter-grade credentials three screens into `/about`, essays about Tinder and family dinners on the front page, YouTube next to LinkedIn. Those are two sites. "I write personal essays because reflecting in public is how I learn" is a hireable sentence. "a web developer who loves to write about lifestyles" is not.
4. **The newest post is December 2023 and the h1 says "Latest."** An undated "Essays" archive ages gracefully; a page labelled "Latest" showing a two-year-old post reads as abandonment.
5. **Ten of your fifteen content files belong to a stranger**, and every post links visitors to the repo. What does a recruiter who clicks "View on GitHub" conclude?
