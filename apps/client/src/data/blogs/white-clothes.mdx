I accidentally wore white trousers, a white shirt and a white sweater on the same day, and spent the rest of it thinking about dirt.

On white, every stain shows. On dark colours you can pretend.

Take my lab coat. It is five years old and covered in colourful marks from chemicals and blood — chemistry lab and biology lab. None of them come out, so I have stopped trying. Green, orange, blue, red painted across a white coat is a strange look. On one hand it reads as dirty and inappropriate. On the other, every mark is a record of something I did.

So here is the idea: wear white deliberately, as a way of saving memories from my journeys. Some stains fade with time, but I would still be able to pull something back from each one. That sounds nice to me.

Hygienically I am confident small faded marks after washing change nothing. How much it affects how I *look* is another question. (Obviously I am not wearing the stained ones to anything formal.)

---

A second clothing thought, related.

I realised I have not thought about fashion in years. I cannot remember the last time I cared about looking good. Appropriate, comfortable, not weird — that has been the entire specification.

Then today I caught myself in a mirror and thought, unprompted: "ใส่ชุดขาวแบบนี้ เราดูดีจัง."

The instant I registered that I had thought I looked handsome, I felt profoundly awkward.

I think I know why. Somewhere along the line I adopted a rule about not judging people on appearance. The reasoning was that if I let myself rank people as สวย / หล่อ / หุ่นดี, that ranking would leak into how I treat them, at a level below what I could consciously control. So I trained myself to ignore appearance entirely — and the easiest way to do that was to stop caring about my own.

Logically the rule holds up. Emotionally, is it weird?

And what happens if I start believing I am good-looking? What does that actually cost? (Comparatively speaking I am probably not, since everyone around me clearly takes better care of their appearance than I do.)

Thinking about this is fun and also slightly dangerous, since the obvious endpoint is turning into a narcissist.

Or maybe this is just what growing up feels like.
