I know this is a random thing to be thinking about at my age. But after visiting a certain coffee shop and then having dinner with my aunt and uncle, I couldn't stop asking: what should I do in my retirement?

They seem to have achieved everything, so let me summarise their lives.

During the Tom Yum Kung crisis, both of them started working as cruise ship crew with no financial help from anyone. From there they became flight attendants, which is how they ended up seeing most of the world on someone else's schedule. Later my uncle moved into hotels and eventually ran a department at an international chain — managing hundreds of people, earning accordingly. They retired in their forties.

Then here is what they did with it.

They sold the house in the city. Sold the BMW. Sold most of the luxurious things. They bought land in the countryside and a second-hand pickup truck, and over one or two years turned a grass field into houses and farms — learning how to build as they built. That is cool as hell.

They are not the only people I have met like this. Money, connections, knowledge, travel — the full set. And the conclusion at the end keeps being the same: there was no need to make something impactful, or change the world, or get richer. Just live peacefully and be kind to people.

I think I would be fine with that path.

---

So here are mine, assuming the happy paths where I get lucky:

**1. Single**

- 1.1 Travel — backpacking, indefinitely
- 1.2 Peaceful — maybe a small cafe or shop somewhere
- 1.3 Contribute — build something that matters (I genuinely don't know what)

**2. Married** — mostly depends on my wife

- 2.1 With children
- 2.2 Without children

And one more, for after: sell everything I own and turn it into scholarships for random smart kids to study abroad. No inheritance for relatives or children. I find this idea more appealing the longer I sit with it.

I have tested every version of plan 1 in small doses, and I can honestly say I could enjoy any of them to the end. I want to try more of each first.

Plan 2 is where it falls apart. I cannot picture myself in a relationship at all. Maybe I am not mature enough — there is a lot I still need to fix. So bad at this game, T_T.

What strikes me writing this out is that the two branches barely interact. Everything under "single" is something I can start practising this weekend. Everything under "married" waits on a person who doesn't exist yet in my life, and who would then get a vote on all of it. I don't think I mind that. I just notice that I've planned one branch in detail and left the other as a stub.

I want to write about each plan properly — how I think about it, why I'd choose it. But not today. I have already spent two or three hours thinking and writing all of this. Reflecting on yourself is very fun and it is a complete time sink.
