Let me get serious and talk about my childhood for a bit.

If I remember right, my parents raised me in primary school on a simple rule: _ถ้าลูกอยากทำอะไร ก็พร้อมสนับสนุน_.

When I decided I wanted to be a pilot, that meant going to a flight school open day, trying a flight simulator, sitting as copilot in a small aircraft, and going up into a real ATC tower. In primary school. I was taking it seriously.

_[This is the view I was chasing as a kid — photo by Olga Ernst, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cessna_182D_Skylane_Cockpit.jpg)_

I lost interest in flying eventually and became a kid who simply did whatever he wanted. Running around, football, video games, music, writing novels, cooking. Whatever was in front of me.

That freedom meant I ran into an enormous amount of life very early. Good and bad.

The bad list is genuinely bad. Skipping class. Falling in with the wrong friends. Bullying others, and being on the wrong side of the way kids talk about each other. Fights with other classes. Friends showing me things no primary schooler should be looking at. Situations I did not have the vocabulary to handle. And worse than that.

The good list is much shorter and I struggled to remember any of it. The one I've got: one afternoon, waiting for my mum to pick me up, I noticed how much rubbish there was, and just started picking it up around the school until she arrived. (Almost nobody used the bins back then. You finished something, you dropped it.)

That's my primary school.

---

Looking back at it, the thing I feel is not pride. It's luck.

I happened to fall in with a good core group of friends. I happened to have very good teachers. My parents had taught me enough, early enough, that I could still tell things apart. And I learned quickly enough to test into a middle school with a healthy environment around it.

All four of those things had to hold for me to come out able to distinguish and control myself.

Now flip it. It would have been so easy for me to go the other way at that age. If I had, I might be breaking the law right now, or in prison, or a father at nineteen. Not as a dramatic hypothetical — as a plausible alternate branch that was one or two friendships away.

(Secondary school, by contrast, was nothing. เรียนพิเศษ 24/7, bruh.)

That freedom is why I am who I am today. I genuinely don't know whether that's good or bad on net. What I do know is that it made me see the world much more widely, and understand people — good ones and bad ones — much better than I otherwise would have.

---

Which leads to the thing I have actually been obsessed with since high school: **การเลี้ยงลูก**.

Define สุดยอด, for my purposes, as: เข้าใจคนอื่น คิดถึงคนอื่น และพยายามช่วยเหลือทำให้คนอื่นมีความสุข.

Every time I meet a friend or a person who is สุดยอด, I ask myself the same two questions. _พ่อแม่เลี้ยงมายังไงให้สุดยอดขนาดนี้?_ And _โตขึ้นไปคนนั้นจะสุดยอดขนาดไหน และจะสร้างครอบครัวและเลี้ยงลูกต่อยังไง?_

I don't think I'm สุดยอด yet — I am still selfish as hell. But I want to get as close as I can. And I want to talk to those people, and if possible, to their parents.

I have no conclusion. The questions I keep getting stuck on are:

- How do you know something is the right thing for a child?
- What is "raising" actually doing? How much freedom should a child get?
- Everyone points to MIT portfolios, ISEF winners, Olympiad students as proof of good parenting. But only a handful of kids are like that. For the rest, forcing it isn't parenting — they should be the ones choosing how they live.
- And yet: how a child chooses comes from how they were raised. So where does the choosing actually start?

I have been invested in this since high school and I have not reached a single good conclusion. So I keep writing about it.

The honest version of my ambition is smaller than it sounds. I'm not trying to solve parenting. I want to understand people well enough that if someone near me is unhappy, I would notice. Right now everyone around me seems fine — but if one of them wasn't, would I be able to tell?

The answer is just to keep understanding more. แงง ทำยังไงให้คนรอบตัวมีความสุขทุกคนอะ ยากจัง.
