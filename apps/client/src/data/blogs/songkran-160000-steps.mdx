Bangkok is full of places you can reach on foot or by public transport, so during Songkran I decided to backpack around a city I already live in.

The method was simple, and I recommend it:

1. Find and book a hostel for tonight.
2. Find places worth seeing along the path to that hostel.
3. Walk to them, arrive at the hostel whenever I arrive.
4. Sleep. Repeat.

No itinerary beyond one day. That constraint is what made the trip.

### Day one

Samyan Mitrtown → Mahanakhon → Taksin Bridge → IconSiam (by boat) → back to Taksin → Asiatique (by boat) → House of Phraya Jasaen Hostel.

I skipped the Mahanakhon observation deck — around 1,000 baht felt like poor value for the day I was having. The parks under both ends of Taksin Bridge turned out to be the better find. IconSiam was luxurious enough that all I could do was feel outmatched by everything in it. The river at night from the boat was the real attraction.

At Asiatique I caught a live piano-and-violin duet from a restaurant — the chord progression was adorable — and rode the ferris wheel, which was frightening for about thirty seconds and then wonderful. My first time in a hostel, and the staff were so nice.

**Awkward moment:** walking back through dark quiet streets, a Korean couple happened to be going my way. I felt weird walking that close behind them, so I sped up and passed them. Then I went into a 7-Eleven — and they walked in too. Then another 7-Eleven, a while later. Same thing. I last saw them one block from my hostel. I still don't know whether they noticed. I definitely did.

### Day two

Hostel → IconSiam (by boat) → Lhong 1919 → every nostalgic place near Suan Kularb → the Giant Swing → Old City Wall and Golden Mountain → Khaosan Road → LOBSUEK Hostel.

Woke at 11, too excited about the hostel to have slept properly. Lhong 1919 was the surprise — I did not expect a Chinese historical site sitting in the middle of Bangkok. Then I walked through my old middle school area and discovered how much had changed since I left. Khaosan was too crowded for me, though I learned a lot there (most of it strictly 20+).

### Day three

Hostel → Rama VIII Bridge → Feel Good Hostel (walk-in booking) → Royal Barges National Museum → Wat Arun → Tha Tian and Museum Siam → everything around the Grand Palace → back over Rama VIII.

The walk from the main road to the Royal Barges Museum — houses and canals along narrow paths — was the most pleasant stretch of the whole trip. At Wat Arun there was a pop band playing "ดาว" by Pause, which I love, and a monk giving a talk using words like _soft skill_ and _fast-changing world_. Museum Siam had an exhibition on "What is Thainess," on how Thai culture became what it is.

**Three awkward moments, all the same shape:**

At the hostel someone asked "คนไทยไหมครับ". After speaking English for days straight I answered "Yeah" — then reflexively added "ครับ".

At the Royal Barges Museum I'd bought my ticket in English, so I kept speaking English through a five-minute pitch for a canal boat tour. I declined; I already had plans. Walking away I heard him tell the other staff, "แหม่ หลอกไม่สำเร็จ". I felt bad for letting him think I was a tourist — though I am still curious how the same conversation would have gone if he'd known I was Thai.

At Wat Arun someone asked "คนไทยไหมครับ" from far enough away that I just nodded. He asked again. I nodded again. Then: "ขอดูบัตรประชาชนได้ไหมครับ". The moment my hand moved toward my pocket — "โอเคๆ ไปได้ๆ". I hadn't even known there was a foreigner entrance fee.

And then, at 2 a.m., I left my keycard in the room while going to the bathroom. I had to call a staff member in the middle of the night. The door code didn't work, so she talked me through finding the master key hidden at the front counter. (Seriousness aside: first time in my life I've talked to a woman for a full hour.)

### Day four

Hostel → Central Pinklao → Democracy Monument → Royal Park → The Old Siam → Mega Plaza Saphan Lek → Ong Ang Canal → China Town → Bangkok Train Station → Samyan Mitrtown.

The staff came to check I was okay after the keycard incident, which genuinely moved me. My laundry was still wet from the humidity, I missed the hostel breakfast window, and ended up with a scone and coffee from somewhere nearby.

Mega Plaza Saphan Lek shocked me — an enormous building of video games, card games, figures, anime CDs, BB guns, toys. They had Contra for the NES and PS1 games. I think of collecting as "just a hobby" and then you see how big the room is.

Ong Ang Canal was the best thing I saw in four days. The graffiti, the design, the buildings — all of it detailed and deliberate. It was romantic enough that I caught myself imagining walking it with a girlfriend, which is not a thought I normally have.

### Journey complete

- Walked: **160,000 steps** (50+ km)
- Boats: 4 · Buses: 1
- Places visited: **131**
- Total spend: ~3,000 baht (food + hostels + tickets)
- Hostels only: 1,200 baht
- Slept: 16 hours across three nights (7 + 4 + 5)
- Sunburn: 7 spots (_I wore sunscreen_ T_T)
- Beautiful girls found: 0

**Score: 8/10.**

These are simple trips. But for a kid who was เรียนพิเศษ 24/7 from a very young age, walking my own city with nowhere to be was genuinely eye-opening. I have loved walking and backpacking ever since.
