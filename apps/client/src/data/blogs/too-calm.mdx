Something very bad happened to me today, and I was so calm about it that the calmness itself scared me.

For most of my life I have quietly observed how I react to serious things — near-death moments, furious moments, depressed moments, huge quarrels. What I found is a little strange.

Normally my reaction scales with the event. A small annoyance gets a small reaction, a big one gets a big reaction. But past a certain threshold, the curve inverts. The reaction gets _smaller_. Above that line there is no anger, no panic, no fear, no relief. Just calm.

And in that state, everything sharpens. Perceiving, analysing, executing — all of it runs past my normal limits. It feels like god mode. Like being in the zone.

I don't know when this started. I think I use it unconsciously because somewhere along the way I learned how badly things go when you lose control. What I don't know is whether it actually works, or whether I have just never been around long enough afterward to see the bill.

Try to picture it from the outside. Imagine me dead furious about something. Then, one second later, I am speaking in a monotone and looking at you with no expression at all.

Isn't that scary?

Here is what I keep circling back to. Calmness in a crisis is genuinely useful — it is the thing that lets you act instead of freeze. But there is a difference between _feeling_ something and choosing not to be run by it, and not feeling it at all. From the inside I honestly cannot tell which one I am doing. The emotion doesn't arrive late. It doesn't arrive at all.

So: is this the right way to deal with these things? Is it normal? And is there a point where all of it comes back at once, as one sea of everything I skipped?

I don't have an answer. I just wanted it written down, so that if the answer shows up later, I have a record of the question.
